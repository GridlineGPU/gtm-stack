# Message guide

Personalization earns its place by explaining fit. Prefer product capability, documented workflow, relevant partnership model, or current role remit. Avoid generic praise and unrelated funding announcements. Keep evidence links on the review card, not necessarily in the message. Recheck dates and named roles before manual action.

Email default: 80–130 words, brief subject, sender/offer, one company-specific relevance sentence, concrete proposal, one ask. Treat length as a preference. LinkedIn invite should fit the limit shown by the current UI; if unknown, draft under 200 characters and flag verification. LinkedIn message can be longer but should not repeat an email verbatim.

## Customer example structure (fill only from evidence)

Subject: [Relevant workflow] at [Prospect]

Hi [Name],

I'm [Sender] from [Company]. We help [verified target users] [actual outcome/capability].

I saw [specific sourced fact about prospect]. That made me think [specific use case] could be worth exploring with your team.

[One approved proof point, or omit if unavailable.]

Would a [short call/demo] be useful to see whether this fits?

[Sender]

## Provider example structure

Subject: [Provider] × [Company] — [specific partnership]

Hi [Name],

I'm [Sender], building [Company]. We [actual product description].

Your [verified capability/program] looks relevant to [specific integration or distribution use case]. We'd like to explore [bounded proposal], with [relevant responsibilities] agreed up front.

Would you be open to [one specific next step]?

[Sender]

If application submission is confirmed, optionally add its exact program name and date. Otherwise omit the claim entirely.

## Follow-ups

First: add one useful detail or clarify scope, then repeat one ask. Final: courteously close loop or ask for correct owner. Avoid fake urgency, guilt, or a stack of messages across channels. Every follow-up is a fresh review item and depends on a confirmed earlier send plus current reply check.

## Application preparation

Record actual form URL, field names, required fields and answers; distinguish inspected form fields from a generic proposed description. Do not invent phone numbers, revenue, legal entity, usage estimates or customer commitments. Flag terms/consent choices for the person submitting. No account creation, terms acceptance or submission occurs in this workflow.
